# First non-founder recurring customer

Owner: Elena  
Primary vertical: managed service providers  
Challenger: marketing agencies  
Offer: existing GEO-Pulse free audit and $39/month monitoring subscription  
Revenue condition: an active, recurring Stripe subscription from a customer who is not Jack/Lifter, an internal account, or a test payment

## The operating thesis

The current bottleneck is not audit capacity. It is the transition from an opened scorecard to a
human conversation and then to a recurring monitoring decision.

Run one coherent MSP path:

1. Public evidence-backed contact reason.
2. Free scorecard with preserved campaign attribution.
3. One explicit choice: inspect the report or request a focused walkthrough.
4. Human follow-up for every walkthrough or substantive reply.
5. Existing monitoring checkout only when recurring measurement fits the buyer's need.

Do not widen to a third vertical until an MSP or agency signal reaches a qualified conversation,
checkout start, or active subscription.

## Daily sales block

Every business day:

1. Review new walkthrough requests and inbound reply classifications first.
2. Respond to positive and neutral replies before adding more top-of-funnel inventory.
3. Verify that every active prospect has:
   - a public, truthful reason for selection;
   - a source URL when the reason is used for personalization;
   - a sequence step and next-send time;
   - an owner, next action, and closure condition.
4. Replenish MSP inventory only when less than 14 days of the configured safe cadence remains.
5. Inspect one conversion stage, not just send volume:
   - no deliveries: list/contact quality;
   - deliveries but no opens: subject/from/domain reputation;
   - opens but no clicks or walkthroughs: promise and CTA;
   - scans but no conversation: interpretation and assisted path;
   - conversations but no checkout: qualification, monitoring need, or trust;
   - checkout without subscription: checkout reliability or offer fit.

## Qualification

A qualified MSP has:

- a public business website;
- a real managed IT, cybersecurity, cloud, or support offer;
- a reachable owner, sales leader, or marketing decision-maker using a public business address;
- a recurring need to understand and report visibility;
- enough public site evidence for GEO-Pulse to produce a useful baseline;
- no suppression, unsubscribe, consent, geography, or policy conflict.

Disqualify generic directories, parked domains, personal addresses without a public business role,
sites that block the promised audit, and businesses for which the recurring measurement has no
credible use.

## Focused walkthrough

Target: 20 minutes. The point is diagnosis and fit, not a feature tour.

### Opening

"I reviewed the public site and the audit. Before I interpret it, what buyer question or service
line matters most to you right now?"

### Discovery

Ask only what changes the recommendation:

1. Which services and locations are the commercial priority?
2. How are new customers finding and evaluating the company today?
3. Has anyone checked what ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity says for those buyer questions?
4. Who owns website changes?
5. Would a monthly baseline be used internally, with clients, or not at all?

### Evidence review

- Show one observed pass to establish that the audit is not manufactured negativity.
- Show no more than two high-confidence gaps.
- Explain the evidence and boundary before the implication.
- Do not claim that a fix will cause a citation, ranking, lead, or revenue result.

### Close

If recurring measurement fits:

"A single audit is the snapshot. The existing monitoring plan is $39 per month and rechecks the
baseline on the documented cadence. If that ongoing comparison would be useful, we can start it
from this report. If not, keep the audit and use the fixes."

If it does not fit, do not force checkout. Record the reason and close the lead.

## Objection handling

### "We already do SEO."

"Good. GEO-Pulse does not replace technical SEO. It adds an audit and measurement layer for the
public signals and answer-engine observations your existing work can influence."

### "Can you guarantee ChatGPT will recommend us?"

"No. GEO-Pulse reports observable site signals and measured prompt results. It does not control or
guarantee rankings, citations, traffic, or inclusion."

### "Why pay monthly?"

"Only pay monthly if the comparison is useful: what changed, what regressed, and what the configured
buyer questions returned over time. If a one-time snapshot is enough, the free audit can remain the
right next step."

### "Send information."

Send the existing report and annotated example. Set one explicit follow-up date. Do not restart a
generic sequence around an active conversation.

### "Not now."

Ask whether the problem is timing or fit. If timing, schedule one consent-aligned follow-up. If fit,
close the lead.

## Warm introductions and referrals

Warm intros are a separate lane from cold outreach. Do not enroll them in an automated sequence.

Build a list of:

- founder relationships with MSP owners, consultants, vendors, or local business groups;
- current users who can truthfully introduce one separate business;
- partners who already advise MSPs on web, SEO, cybersecurity marketing, or operations.

Draft intro request:

> I am looking for one MSP owner who would give us a candid look at a public-site AI-search audit.
> The audit is free; the paid product is the existing $39/month monitoring option only if the
> recurring baseline is genuinely useful. Would you be comfortable introducing one person who fits?

Draft referred-buyer note:

> [Introducer] suggested I show you a GEO-Pulse audit. It checks the public signals that help search
> and AI systems access and understand an MSP website. I can run the free baseline first, then walk
> you through the two clearest findings. There is no ranking or citation guarantee.

These are draft external messages. Use only through the existing approval contract for novel
one-to-one communications.

## Experiment contract

Current experiment: MSP evidence-first page and walkthrough CTA.

- Audience: qualified MSP contacts only.
- Primary variable: assisted walkthrough CTA versus report-only CTA.
- Attribution campaign: `msp-first-customer`.
- Success: at least one matched reply or walkthrough request from a qualified MSP; downstream
  success is a recurring subscription.
- Guardrail: no unsupported claim, no increase to sending or spend caps, no third vertical.
- Stop/revise: after a meaningful delivered sample with opens but no replies, change one of the
  promise, CTA, or landing proof—not all three at once.

## Closure rules

- Positive or neutral human reply: stop automation, owner responds personally.
- Unsubscribe: stop immediately and preserve suppression.
- Wrong person: stop; qualify a different public business contact separately.
- Out of office: pause; resume only after manual review.
- Conversion: stop all prospect follow-ups and verify onboarding, baseline, report delivery, and
  active recurring Stripe state.
- Sequence completed without signal: close or recycle only through a separately defined experiment.
