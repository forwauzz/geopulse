# Audience language, sales clarity, and CTA

The post should sound like a useful operator talking to one buyer, not an AI explaining AI.

## Audience brief

Name one:

- MSP owner or operator
- agency owner
- SEO consultant
- founder
- growth marketer
- in-house content lead

Then name the moment:

- preparing for a prospect call
- explaining visibility to a client
- checking why competitors appear in AI answers
- deciding what to fix on the website
- proving whether a change improved measured visibility

Generic “business owner” copy is a fallback, not a default.

## Copy rules

- Lead with a fact, question, or recognizable buying moment.
- Use you, your site, your service, and the buyer’s real task.
- Explain technical terms in the same sentence or remove them.
- Prefer short sentences and familiar words.
- Use one number per frame unless the post is explicitly a data carousel.
- Make the implication concrete: what should the reader check, change, or ask next?
- Sound direct and conversational, but clean up rushed-chat spelling for public copy.

Avoid:

- “AI can’t find you unless…”
- “dominate AI search”
- “future-proof your business”
- “unlock exponential growth”
- “the AI revolution is here”
- vague fear without evidence
- padded category education
- calling a readiness score a ranking
- implying that one technical fix guarantees model inclusion

## Evidence-to-action structures

### Market statistic

1. verified statistic
2. relatable buyer moment
3. what they can measure
4. direct CTA

Example pattern:

> More than [verified number] people use [platform] each [period]. When one of them asks for a
> [service] in [market], is your business part of the answer? Run a free audit to see what the
> measured answers currently surface.

### Audit finding

1. what the audit observed
2. why the issue matters in plain language
3. one practical fix
4. CTA to inspect the reader’s own site

### Benchmark signal

1. the frozen frame and denominator
2. the observed difference
3. the likely operational lesson, labeled as an interpretation
4. CTA to measure the reader’s own baseline

### Outreach asset

1. name the role/problem
2. offer one useful observation
3. show the evidence artifact or method
4. invite one low-friction next step

## CTA ladder

Choose one CTA that matches the asset:

- **Awareness:** Save this checklist.
- **Education:** Check this on your site today.
- **Consideration:** Run the free GEO-Pulse audit.
- **Benchmark:** Measure your current baseline.
- **Outreach:** Reply with your domain and we’ll point you to the right scan.
- **Conversion:** Run the free audit via the link in bio.

Default conversion CTA is direct:

> Run the free audit via the link in bio.

Do not stack “save, share, comment, follow, DM, and click.”

## Instagram contract

- On-screen hook: ideally 3–10 words.
- Static post: one claim and one visual hierarchy.
- Carousel: 4–6 slides; hook, proof, meaning, action, CTA.
- Reel: 8–20 seconds unless the idea genuinely needs more; hook by 1.5 seconds.
- Caption: lead with the takeaway, add the evidence context, then the CTA.
- Hashtags: 3–5 relevant tags; do not use them as a substitute for clear copy.

## LinkedIn contract

- Open with the operational tension, not a generic announcement.
- Give enough methodology to make the evidence credible.
- Separate observation from interpretation.
- Use short paragraphs and one clear takeaway.
- End with the same single CTA, adapted to LinkedIn.

LinkedIn may carry more denominator and method detail than the Instagram graphic, but it must not make
a stronger claim.

## Final comprehension test

Without seeing the brief, a buyer should be able to answer:

1. Is this about me?
2. What did GEO-Pulse or the source actually observe?
3. Why does it matter?
4. What should I do next?

If any answer is unclear, revise before design.
